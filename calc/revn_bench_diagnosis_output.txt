== C. 6820ZZ running torque, properly fitted (SKF frictional-moment model, rolling term; ZZ shields add none)
  dm 112.5 mm, Fa 10.0 N, Fr 2.0 N -> alpha_F 3.8 deg, G_rr 0.0984
  M_rr = G_rr (nu n)^0.6 [N mm]; nu = grease base-oil viscosity at room temperature (ASSUMED 100-250 mm2/s)
  rpm:            100     150     188     225
  nu 100 mm2/s:      25     32     36     40   mN m
  nu 150 mm2/s:      32     40     46     51   mN m
  nu 250 mm2/s:      43     55     63     70   mN m
  The design assumed 1.9 mN m (mu 0.0015 x load), which ignores this viscous rolling term. A thin-section bearing at
  almost no load is all viscous drag: ~30-60 mN m here, more than the whole 29 mN m budget. It drops ~30 % after
  an hour's run-in (grease channels) and ~4x if the grease is replaced by light oil (M ~ nu^0.6).
  Hand-spin coast, bare rotor (I_zz ~1.8e-03 kg m2 ASSUMED) from 1 rev/s at 35 mN m: stops in 0.32 s after 0.16 rev.
  So 'it does not coast' is normal for this bearing. The current at 12 V is the test, not the coast.

== D. The thin inner ring vs a printed spigot
  Inner ring EI 6.71e+06 N mm2; two opposite forces P ovalise it by 3.0 um per N
  Internal clearance (group N) 12-36 um is used up by P = 4-12 N across the ring
  Printed spigot as a free 2.8 mm ring: 210 um per N (soft); the whole rotor ring in its plane: 0.43 um per N
  The rotor ring is 7x stiffer in-plane than the steel inner ring: its 0.4 mm of ovality is imposed on the bearing.
  Measured ovality of the printed ring: 0.40 mm (99.24-99.64). That is 11-33x the bearing's clearance.
  => any spigot that grips on all 8 ribs pinches the balls along its long axis; any that does not pinch is loose on its short axis.

== F. Side loads on the hub: one driving pin at r 8 mm + the belt
  rotor drag   29 mN m: pin force   3.6 N (turns with the rotor), belt effective pull   2.3 N + ~8 N static (fixed)
  rotor drag   60 mN m: pin force   7.5 N (turns with the rotor), belt effective pull   4.7 N + ~8 N static (fixed)
  rotor drag  130 mN m: pin force  16.2 N (turns with the rotor), belt effective pull  10.2 N + ~8 N static (fixed)
  The 6802 is one 5 mm bearing with the load ~10 mm above its centre: the resultant swings 8-30 N once per turn
  at high drag, so the hub nods in the bearing's internal play (~0.2-0.5 deg for a 6802, group N). That is the 'wobble'.

== H. Hanging slip ring: pendulum, wind-up and kinking
  pendulum 50 mm: 2.23 Hz =   134 rpm
  pendulum 60 mm: 2.04 Hz =   122 rpm
  pendulum 68 mm: 1.91 Hz =   115 rpm
  pendulum 80 mm: 1.76 Hz =   106 rpm
  Tonight's top speed was 115-119 rpm: on the pendulum's resonance (68 mm -> 114 rpm).
  red/black pair, 60 mm, twisted  2 turns: shortens  0.8 mm before it kinks
  red/black pair, 60 mm, twisted  5 turns: shortens  4.7 mm before it kinks
  red/black pair, 60 mm, twisted 10 turns: shortens 14.0 mm before it kinks
  kink (loop) torque 2 sqrt(EI T) at 0.10 N tension: 0.89 mN m (6 x 30 AWG PTFE leads of the design: ~4 mN m)
  kink (loop) torque 2 sqrt(EI T) at 0.15 N tension: 1.10 mN m (6 x 30 AWG PTFE leads of the design: ~4 mN m)
  kink (loop) torque 2 sqrt(EI T) at 0.30 N tension: 1.55 mN m (6 x 30 AWG PTFE leads of the design: ~4 mN m)
  A capsule ring's drag is ~1 mN m by catalogue, more on cheap clones (ASSUMED): at or above the pair's kink torque.
  The body is held only by its stator leads in a loose 60 mm loop: nothing stops it turning for the first turn(s),
  so the rotor drags the whole ring round and winds the stator leads; once they wind up, the pair above it twists.
  Both wind-ups shorten the leads and lift the ring. The ring weighs ~10 g: 0.1 N cannot keep 60 mm of lead straight.
