C. Driving the slip ring's rotor: a wire pair vs a printed mast
====================================================================================================
  Two wires twisted between fixed ends: each becomes a helix; torque ~ EA r alpha^3 (alpha = helix angle), strain = alpha^2 / 2.
  assumed rev m.2/n                      1.0 mN m: alpha  2.7 deg, copper strain 0.11 % (yield ~0.1 %), twist over 6 mm    20 deg
  Moflon catalog (0.005 N m)             5.0 mN m: alpha  4.6 deg, copper strain 0.32 % (yield ~0.1 %), twist over 6 mm    34 deg
  A2V CTC012-06S catalog (0.05 N m)     50.0 mN m: alpha  9.9 deg, copper strain 1.48 % (yield ~0.1 %), twist over 6 mm    74 deg
  Greenhill buckling of a free  6.0 mm pair: M_cr = 2 pi EI / L = 2.1 mN m
  Greenhill buckling of a free 12.0 mm pair: M_cr = 2 pi EI / L = 1.0 mN m
  Greenhill buckling of a free 15.0 mm pair: M_cr = 2 pi EI / L = 0.8 mN m
  -> above ~1 mN m the leads yield or buckle; the catalog drag is 5-50 mN m. A mast keyed in the hub's neck carries the torque; the leads ride inside it.
  Offset: wires bent by an axis offset e over L see rotating bending each turn: strand strain ~ 0.04 mm / (L^2 / 6e).
     e 0.3 mm over 10 mm: bend radius  55.6 mm -> strand strain 0.07 % per turn (copper endurance ~0.06-0.09 %)
     e 0.6 mm over 10 mm: bend radius  27.8 mm -> strand strain 0.14 % per turn (copper endurance ~0.06-0.09 %)
  -> the stator floats +-0.4 on its pins so the boss stays on the hub's axis and the leads stay straight.
